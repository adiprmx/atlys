# AI Phrases to Avoid

Words, phrases, and punctuation patterns commonly associated with AI-generated text. Avoid these when they make the writing sound canned or unnatural. Context matters: keep a listed term when it is accurate, necessary, or part of the user's voice.

## Em Dashes

AI-written prose often overuses the em dash (—) for emphasis, interruption, or parenthetical information.

### What to do instead

| Instead of | Consider |
|---|---|
| The results—which were surprising—showed... | The results, which were surprising, showed... |
| This approach—unlike traditional methods—allows... | This approach, unlike traditional methods, allows... |
| The study found—as expected—that... | The study found, as expected, that... |
| Communication skills—both written and verbal—are essential | Communication skills (both written and verbal) are essential |

Prefer commas for ordinary parenthetical information, colons for explanations or lists, parentheses for supplementary information, and separate sentences when they improve clarity. Keep an em dash when its emphasis or interruption is deliberate.

## Overused transitions and connectors

| Avoid when padded | Consider |
|---|---|
| furthermore | also, in addition, and |
| moreover | also, and, besides |
| notwithstanding | despite, even so, still |
| that being said | however, but, still |
| at its core | essentially, fundamentally |
| to put it simply | in short |
| it is worth noting that | note that, importantly |
| in the realm of | in, within, regarding |
| in the landscape of | in, within |
| in today's [anything] | currently, now, today |

## Phrases that can signal AI writing

### Openings

- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In today's digital age..."
- "In an era of..."
- "In the ever-evolving landscape of..."
- "In the realm of..."
- "It's important to note that..."
- "Let's delve into..."
- "Imagine a world where..."

### Transitions

- "That being said..."
- "With that in mind..."
- "It's worth mentioning that..."
- "At its core..."
- "To put it simply..."
- "In essence..."
- "This begs the question..."

### Conclusions

- "In conclusion..."
- "To sum up..."
- "By [doing X], you can [achieve Y]..."
- "In the final analysis..."
- "All things considered..."
- "At the end of the day..."

### Structural patterns

- "Whether you're a [X], [Y], or [Z]..." when the three examples add no value
- "It's not just [X], it's also [Y]..."
- "Think of [X] as [elaborate metaphor]..."
- Repeatedly starting sentences with "By" followed by a gerund
- Repeated sets of exactly three adjectives, benefits, examples, or clauses

## Academic-specific patterns

| Avoid when padded | Consider |
|---|---|
| shed light on | clarify, explain, reveal |
| pave the way for | enable, allow, make possible |
| a myriad of | many, numerous, various |
| a plethora of | many, numerous, several |
| paramount | important, essential, critical |
| pertaining to | about, regarding, concerning |
| prior to | before |
| subsequent to | after |
| in light of | because of, given, considering |
| with respect to | about, regarding, for |
| in terms of | regarding, for, about |
| the fact that | that, or rewrite the sentence |

## Self-check

1. Read the text aloud. Revise phrases that sound unnatural in conversation with a colleague.
2. Check for repetitive sentence structures.
3. Look for clusters of the patterns above rather than treating one word as proof of weak writing.
4. Vary sentence length only where it improves rhythm.
5. Confirm that each hedge and intensifier changes the meaning.
